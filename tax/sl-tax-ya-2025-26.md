# Sri Lanka Personal Income Tax — Y/A 2025/26 Reference

Year of assessment: **1 April 2025 – 31 March 2026**. Researched 16 Oct 2026.

Governing law: Inland Revenue Act No. 24 of 2017, as amended by **Act No. 02 of 2025**
(certified 20 Mar 2025, effective 1 Apr 2025) and **Act No. 11 of 2026** (certified
3 Jun 2026; several provisions apply retrospectively from 1 Apr 2025).

Primary sources (IRD):
- Notice PN/IT/2025-01 (26 Mar 2025): Act No. 2 of 2025 changes

## 1. Deadlines for Y/A 2025/26

| Item | Due | Payment code |
|---|---|---|
| Instalment 1 | 15 Aug 2025 | 25261 |
| Instalment 2 | 15 Nov 2025 | 25262 |
| Instalment 3 | 15 Feb 2026 | 25263 |
| Instalment 4 | 15 May 2026 | 25264 |
| Final payment of balance tax | **30 Sep 2026** (already passed) | 25260 |
| Return of income + Statement of Assets & Liabilities | **30 Nov 2026** | — |

- E-filing is mandatory (IRD e-Services portal). Senior citizens may file on paper from 2025/26.
- A "Simplified Return" is available if the only income is employment + interest.
- **No return needed** if the only income is employment income fully covered by APIT
  (no instalments or final tax due), optionally plus interest ≤ Rs 5,000 — *unless* IRD has
  already opened a file for you (then you must keep filing). (Act 11/2026, effective 1 Apr 2025)

### Penalties
- Late return: greater of (5% of tax owing + 1%/month) or (Rs 50,000 + Rs 10,000/month after 30 Nov 2026).
- Late instalment: 10% of the unpaid amount. Late tax for the year: 20%. Interest: 1.5% per month.
- False or misleading statement: greater of Rs 50,000 or the tax understated.
- Failing to file: fine up to Rs 1m and/or up to 1 year in prison. Act 11/2026 also adds prosecution after a 30-day notice.
- Interest on late tax up to Y/A 2024/25 is waived if the principal is paid by 2 Dec 2026. This does **not** cover 2025/26.

## 2. Rates and reliefs

**Personal relief:** Rs 1,800,000 (was Rs 1.2m). It is set off against assessable income, but not
against gains from realising investment assets. Residents get it, and so do non-resident Sri Lankan citizens.

**Progressive rates on taxable income** (the 12% band is gone and the first band is wider):

| Taxable income slice | Rate | Cumulative tax at top of slice |
|---|---|---|
| First 1,000,000 | 6% | 60,000 |
| Next 500,000 | 18% | 150,000 |
| Next 500,000 | 24% | 270,000 |
| Next 500,000 | 30% | 420,000 |
| Balance (> 2,500,000) | 36% | 420,000 + 36% × (T − 2.5m) |

In terms of assessable income (after the Rs 1.8m relief), tax starts above Rs 1.8m, and the
36% rate applies above Rs 4.3m.

**Other reliefs (Fifth Schedule):**
- **Rent relief:** 25% of rental income from an investment asset. You get this instead of claiming actual expenses.
- **Solar panel relief:** up to Rs 600,000 a year. It is capped at the cost of panels fixed on your own premises and connected to the national grid, or at the bank loan repayments for them. Any unused cost carries forward year to year, as in the IRD examples. Income from selling electricity to the grid counts as business income.

**Qualifying payments (Schedule 5):**
- Donations to approved charities: the least of the amount donated, Rs 75,000, or ⅓ of taxable income (¼ of assessable income).
- Donations in money or kind to the Government or specified institutions, such as government schools and hospitals. From 2025/26, any unused amount carries forward (Act 11/2026).
- Contributions to set up a shop for a female member of a Samurdhi beneficiary family.
- Film and cinema expenditure (restricted to ⅓ of taxable income).
- The old Rs 1.2m "expenditure relief" (medical, education, housing interest and so on) **no longer exists**. Don't look for these items.

**Special rates:**
- Capital gains (realisation of investment assets): **10%** for 2025/26. It rose to 15% only for realisations from 3 Jun 2026. A CGT return and payment are due within 30 days after the end of the month of disposal.
- Terminal benefits (gratuity, commuted pension, ETF at retirement, approved compensation for loss of office): first Rs 10m at 0%, next Rs 10m at 6%, balance at 12%.
- Betting, gaming, liquor and tobacco business: 45%.

## 3. Foreign income: maximum 15% (the biggest change for 2025/26)

The **exemption** on service exports and foreign-source income was **removed from 1 Apr 2025**.
In its place, the tax on the following income is capped at **15%**:

1. **Service exports:** gains and profits from any service rendered in or outside Sri Lanka, to be used
   outside Sri Lanka, where payment is **received in foreign currency and remitted through a bank to Sri Lanka**.
2. **Foreign-source income:** gains and profits from a foreign source, earned in foreign currency and
   **remitted through a bank to Sri Lanka**.

Points the IRD guide confirms:
- This covers freelancers and contractors (business income) and **remote employees of foreign employers**
  (employment income; IRD Example 6, a software engineer paid in USD by a US company). Employees cannot
  deduct expenses. APIT on foreign employment income uses **APIT Table 08**.
- Foreign income kept offshore or received outside banking channels gets no cap. It is taxed at the normal progressive rates.
- Foreign remittances are declared under their normal income heads (Schedules 1–4). The remitted amounts are also reported in **Cage 210A** (Schedule 4, cage 406e).
- **Computation method** (IRD Annexure 7 and Examples 2, 3, 6):
  - The personal relief and qualifying payments can be set against *either* pool. The IRD says to pick whichever gives the
    lowest tax, which normally means against local income, since that is taxed at up to 36%.
  - Local taxable income goes through the normal progressive bands.
  - Foreign taxable income is taxed at 15%. If foreign income is the only income, the special rate table is 6% on the first
    Rs 1m of taxable income and 15% on the balance.
- **Foreign tax credit** (s.80–81) is limited to the Sri Lankan tax on that foreign income. It cannot be set against other tax, and it is non-refundable.

Worked check (IRD Example 6). Foreign employment Rs 16.38m, LKR FD interest Rs 3.6m, rent Rs 1.08m:
- Local taxable income = 3.6m + 1.08m − 270k rent relief − 1.8m personal relief − 75k donation = 2,535,000. Tax = 432,600.
- Tax on foreign income = 16.38m × 15% = 2,457,000.
- Total = 2,889,600.

## 4. Withholding tax and AIT (from 1 Apr 2025)

**Creditable:** you claim these against your tax in Schedule 7 → Schedule 9. Keep every certificate.

| Payment | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Interest or discount (bank or FI deposits) | **10%** AIT | Was 5%. Not deducted if a valid self-declaration was given (see below). |
| Rent paid to a resident | 10% of the full payment | Only if rent from that payer is over Rs 100,000 per calendar month. |
| Service fees to a resident non-employee individual | 5% of the full payment | Only if the payer pays over Rs 100,000 per calendar month. Covers teaching/lecturing/exams, insurance/sales commissions, and independent professionals (doctor, engineer, accountant, lawyer, software developer, researcher, academic). Act 11/2026 widened the list from 3 Jun 2026; that wider list does not apply to 2025/26. |
| Employment income | APIT per IRD tables | Credit uses the employer's T-10 certificate. |

**Final:** no further tax and no credit. Declare in Schedule 6.

| Payment | Rate |
|---|---|
| Dividends from a resident company | **15% final** |
| Lottery, betting and gambling winnings | 14% final (lottery winnings ≤ Rs 500k are exempt) |

**Self-declaration (interest):** a resident individual whose assessable income from all sources is
**Rs 1.8m or less** can file the IRD form with each bank to stop the 10% AIT. A false declaration carries a penalty of up to Rs 200,000. If AIT was
deducted anyway, you claim it back from IRD through the return, not from the bank. Refunds up to Rs 180,000 are processed
within 3 months of the claim, before audit.

## 5. Exempt or excluded items that often appear in bank statements

- **Interest on foreign-currency accounts** with a commercial or specialised bank (CBSL-approved): exempt, and no AIT is deducted.
- Interest on a "Special Deposit Account" (opened on or after 8 Apr 2020, excluding renewals).
- Interest on foreign-currency sovereign bonds, including Sri Lanka Development Bonds.
- Gains on **listed (CSE) shares**.
- Gain on selling your principal residence (owned 3 years and lived in for 2 of them).
- Small capital gains: each gain ≤ Rs 50k, with total gains for the year ≤ Rs 600k.
- **Life insurance proceeds** on death, maturity or surrender. This is not employment or business income, nor an annuity or pension (Act 11/2026, s.52A, from 1 Apr 2025).
- **Gains on selling a personal motor vehicle** are not income; losses are not deductible (from 1 Apr 2024).
- EPF/ETF and approved provident fund withdrawals at retirement; Government pensions.
- Gifts and transfers between family members are not income, but they must appear in the Statement of Assets & Liabilities (Part 02).
- Moving your own money between accounts (own transfers, FD placements and maturities of principal) is not income.

**Unit trusts:** these are pass-through from 2025/26. Fund certificates (due by 31 Aug 2026) show the income and WHT to declare.
If a fund does not issue a certificate, it is taxed as a company and the unit holder pays nothing.

## 6. Statement of Assets & Liabilities (as at 31 Mar 2026)

This is filed with the return. It needs:
- **Bank balances, including FDs**, at 31.03.2026. These come from the March 2026 statements.
- Shares and securities, vehicles, cash in hand, loans given, and gold/jewellery/**virtual assets**.
- Immovable property in Sri Lanka and overseas.
- Liabilities, including loans and credit-card balances.
- Assets acquired, gifts received, and assets disposed of during 2025/26.

The IRD reconciles the movement in net assets against declared income. Large unexplained deposits are the main audit risk.

## 7. Other changes to know about

- **TIN** is needed to open bank accounts or get credit cards (from 1 Apr 2026) and is no longer confidential.
- **Instalments for 2026/27 onward** are based on the *previous year's* tax, i.e. 2025/26. The Statement of Estimated Tax has been abolished. Q1 2026/27 was due 15 Aug 2026.
- **Residency (from 1 Apr 2025):** a person who leaves for overseas employment under a contract of at least 1 year with an unrelated foreign employer is non-resident for the contract period. Holders of an Investor Category Residence Visa are non-resident.
- **Cash payments** of Rs 500k or more (other than by cheque, card, bank transfer, or deposit into the payee's account) are not deductible as business expenses and cannot be added to an asset's cost.
- **Interest on foreign loans** is exempt only if the money is brought in through a bank in foreign currency and used in Sri Lanka.
- **Refunds** must be claimed within 30 months of the end of the Y/A (so by 30 Sep 2028 for 2025/26).
- **VAT:** registration is required above Rs 60m a year or Rs 15m a quarter of turnover. A proposed cut to Rs 36m was reportedly dropped. This only matters for very high-turnover freelancers.

## 8. Bank statement checklist

Classify each **credit** as one of:
1. **Salary from a local employer.** Schedule 1. Match it to the T-10 / APIT certificate.
2. **Salary in foreign currency from a foreign employer, remitted through a bank.** Schedule 1 with the **15% cap**. Note the conversion rate used.
3. **Freelance or client payments.** Schedule 2 business income:
   - Foreign currency through the bank (Payoneer/Wise/PayPal → SL bank, Upwork, Fiverr, direct SWIFT): **15% cap**.
   - LKR from local clients: progressive rates. Check whether 5% WHT was deducted (only if over Rs 100k/month from that payer).
   - Business expenses are deductible against this income, and records are needed.
4. **Interest.** Schedule 3:
   - LKR interest has 10% AIT, which is creditable.
   - FCY account interest is exempt.
   - Use the **gross** amount (net ÷ 0.9 if only the net is shown).
5. **Rent received.** Schedule 3. Deduct 25% relief, and claim the 10% WHT credit if the tenant deducted it.
6. **Dividends.** 15% final. Schedule 6 only.
7. **Not income:** own transfers, FD principal, loans received, gifts from family, refunds, insurance proceeds, and vehicle sale proceeds. These still matter for the Statement of Assets & Liabilities.

From the **debits**, flag:
- Tax payments to IRD (instalment codes 25261–25264, 25260).
- Approved charity donations and donations to the Government.
- Solar loan repayments and solar purchases.
- Business expenses (freelancers).
- Large asset purchases (these go in the Statement of Assets & Liabilities).

Documents to collect alongside the statements:
- APIT / T-10 certificates.
- AIT certificates from each bank (banks must issue them free).
- WHT certificates from clients and tenants.
- Dividend and unit trust certificates.
- Evidence of foreign-currency inward remittances (bank advices or SWIFT records).
- Proof of the residency and age (senior citizen) position.
